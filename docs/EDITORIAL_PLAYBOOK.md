# Creator Growth Tools — Editorial Playbook v1.0

Last updated: 2026-08-22

## Purpose

Create useful English-language comparison content that answers a real buying question, synthesizes information readers would otherwise gather themselves, separates facts/user feedback/editorial judgment, reads like edited native English, and creates a record that can later be compared with traffic and affiliate results.

## Evidence hierarchy

1. Official vendor product/pricing/help/changelog/legal pages for facts.
2. Verified review marketplaces such as G2 and Capterra for recurring praise/complaints.
3. Reddit and other communities for anecdotal friction and real workflow language.

Never convert one viral complaint into a platform-wide fact.

## Honest review model

Do not write “I tested,” “my experience,” or equivalent unless an actual test occurred.

Allowed framing:
- “Across G2 and Capterra, recurring themes include…”
- “The public review data points to…”
- “Recent verified reviewers frequently mention…”
- “Recent community discussions include reports of…”

Do not claim to have analyzed every review if you only used ratings, summaries, and visible recent examples.

## Originality

Original value comes from synthesis:
- what official sources say;
- what verified reviewers repeatedly like;
- what verified reviewers repeatedly dislike;
- what recent community discussions add;
- which business model the product actually fits;
- what the reader should do differently.

Use concrete scenarios instead of abstract SaaS language.

Weak: “Kit is strong for creator workflows.”

Better: “A coach can offer a free PDF, start a five-email welcome sequence, tag readers who click a workshop link, and later send a launch sequence. That is the type of workflow Kit handles naturally.”

## English style

Prefer short/medium sentences, concrete nouns and verbs, direct recommendations, realistic examples, natural contractions, and varied structure.

Avoid repeated AI-template language such as:
- the key is
- the tradeoff is
- stronger choice
- best fit
- creator-native
- newsletter-native
- in today’s digital landscape
- it’s important to note
- ultimately, the choice depends on

These are not banned; repetition is the problem.

Avoid internal startup/developer language in reader-facing copy unless relevant: MVP, stack, pipeline, operational overhead, CRM-adjacent.

## Structure by search intent

### Ranking article
Quick answer → methodology → shortlist → individual picks → decision framework → FAQ.

### X vs Y
Short answer → methodology → biggest difference → pricing → review consensus by product → use-case comparison → verdict → FAQ.

### Review article
Verdict → methodology/public ratings → what users like → what users dislike → pricing → who it is/isn’t for → alternatives → FAQ.

### Alternatives article
Organize first by the reader’s reason for switching, then map each reason to a product.

## Commercial integrity

Affiliate status must not determine factual conclusions.
No fake experience, fake review, fake screenshot, fake user count, fake price, fake score, or invented affiliate ID.

If an internal score is ever introduced, its formula and source data must be stored.

## Rating snapshots

Whenever an article publishes a rating/review count:
- include a Last Verified date;
- store source/count/rating in the publication log;
- refresh when stale.

## Images

Prefer original diagrams/infographics, our own screenshots where appropriate, and official media assets with permitted usage. Do not scrape random Google Images results.

## SEO / helpful-content rule

Search optimization is not keyword repetition. Every article must answer the query quickly, provide more than vendor-page summaries, include meaningful comparison logic, avoid padding, and have a clear reason to exist.

## Methodology page

Maintain a public Editorial Methodology page explaining evidence hierarchy, no-fake-experience policy, affiliate neutrality, and verification dates.

## Refresh triggers

Refresh when pricing/plan structure changes, affiliate terms change, a major release changes the recommendation, review consensus materially shifts, the article is >90 days old and receiving traffic, or conversion data shows intent mismatch.

## Experiment principle

Every article is content and an experiment. Log search intent, article archetype, primary product, editorial hypothesis, evidence sources, dates, Pinterest mapping, traffic, affiliate clicks, signup/trial/sale, and revision notes.
