## The short answer

Choose **Substack** if you want to start writing today with no fixed cost and you want the platform's own readership to help you get found.

Choose **beehiiv** if you want the publication to be yours — your domain, your growth mechanics, your ad and sponsorship revenue — and you are willing to pay a monthly fee for it.

That is the real fork, and it is not primarily about features. It is about **who carries the risk**. Substack charges you nothing until readers pay you, then takes a share forever. beehiiv charges you every month whether you earn or not, then leaves your subscription revenue alone.

## The biggest difference: what you are actually paying for

Ask what happens the month you earn $0.

On Substack, you pay nothing. Publishing is free, there is no subscriber cap to worry about, and the platform's cut only appears once readers pay you.

On beehiiv, a paid plan bills you the same whether you earned $0 or $10,000.

Now ask what happens the month you earn $5,000 from paid subscriptions.

On Substack, roughly $500 goes to the platform, and card processing comes out on top of that. On beehiiv's Scale plan, the take rate on paid subscriptions is 0% — you pay the monthly fee and keep the subscription revenue.

Neither is generous or greedy. They are two different bets on when you will make money.

## Pricing in 2026

### beehiiv

beehiiv's pricing page uses a subscriber slider. These are the values displayed at the **default 1,000-subscriber position on August 31, 2026**:

- Launch: **$0/month**, up to 2,500 subscribers, unlimited sends, website, custom domain, recommendation network.
- Scale: **$43 per month**, shown as **$517 billed annually** ("Save $71/year"), with a 100,000-subscriber ceiling. Adds the Ad Network, paid Recommendations, 0% take rate on paid subscriptions, automations, and webhooks.
- Max: **$96 per month**, shown as **$1,151 billed annually** ("Save $157/year"). Adds branding removal, the Sponsorship Storefront, up to 10 publications, and the Send API.
- Enterprise: custom pricing above 100,000 subscribers.

The price rises as your list grows. Set the slider to your own subscriber count before you use any of these numbers.

### Substack

Substack's model is a revenue share rather than a subscription. Its official page states that **"writers keep 90% of their revenue minus credit card fees"** — the platform's share is 10%, and card processing is charged separately by the payment processor.

There is no publishing fee and no plan ladder to climb. Card fees vary by country and are set by the processor, not by Substack, so check the current rate for where you are rather than trusting any blog's number, including ours.

### The break-even math

This is the comparison most articles skip, so here it is as plain arithmetic using the two verified figures above.

beehiiv Scale at 1,000 subscribers costs **$517 a year**. Substack's share is **10% of paid revenue**. Those two are equal when your paid-subscription revenue is about **$5,170 a year** — roughly $430 a month.

| Annual paid revenue | Substack's 10% share | beehiiv Scale (1,000 subs) | Cheaper on paper |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $0 | $0 | $517 | Substack |
| $2,000 | $200 | $517 | Substack |
| $5,170 | $517 | $517 | Break-even |
| $10,000 | $1,000 | $517 | beehiiv |
| $50,000 | $5,000 | $517 | beehiiv |

Three caveats before you act on that table. Card processing fees apply on both platforms, so they broadly cancel out of this comparison. beehiiv's fee rises with subscriber count, which pushes the break-even point higher as your list grows. And beehiiv's paid plan also buys the Ad Network and paid recommendations, which can earn money that has nothing to do with subscriptions.

Still, the shape holds: **below the break-even, Substack is cheaper; above it, beehiiv's fixed fee gets better every month, and the gap keeps widening.**

## Growth: two different engines

### Substack grows you through Substack

Substack's advantage is that it is a network as well as a tool. Recommendations between publications, the app, and the platform's own reader surfaces mean a new writer is not starting from a cold, empty list on a domain nobody visits.

The cost of that advantage is that the growth is partly rented. Discovery happens inside someone else's ecosystem, on terms you do not set.

### beehiiv grows you through mechanics you control

beehiiv's growth tools are built into the publication itself: a recommendation network, referral mechanics, publication websites on your own domain, and paid recommendations on the Scale plan.

That is more work. Nobody hands you readers because you exist. But the surface is yours, and the audience relationship is not mediated by a feed you do not own.

### Which engine suits you?

If you have no audience anywhere and no distribution habit, Substack's network is a genuine head start.

If you already bring readers from a podcast, a social following, a course, or an existing list, you do not need to rent discovery. You need a system that converts and monetizes what you already have.

## Monetization beyond subscriptions

Paid subscriptions are only one revenue line, and this is where the platforms separate most sharply.

beehiiv's Scale plan includes the Ad Network and paid Recommendations; Max adds a Sponsorship Storefront. Those are advertising and sponsorship revenue streams built into the publishing tool, and they do not depend on any reader ever paying a subscription.

If your plan is sponsorships and ads rather than paid subscriptions, the break-even table above understates beehiiv's case considerably.

## Ownership and portability

Both platforms let you export your subscriber list, so this is not a lock-in horror story.

What differs is what you leave behind. A publication built on your own domain with your own recommendation flows moves with you. A publication built on network discovery, in-app readership, and platform-native subscriber relationships leaves some of its growth behind when it moves.

If you expect to be on the same platform in five years, this does not matter. If you expect to outgrow it, it matters a lot.

## Editorial and design

Recent community discussions about beehiiv include experienced users reporting friction with the editor and with migrating an established publication. Other users report the opposite. The reports do not settle the question, and we will not pretend they do.

Substack's editor is deliberately minimal. For most writers that is a feature. For anyone who wants heavy layout control, it is a constraint they will hit early.

Our advice is the same either way: **write one real issue in each editor before you commit.** Twenty minutes of that beats every review comparison, including this one.

## Our verdict

### Pick Substack if:

- you are starting from zero and want the platform's readership to help;
- you want no fixed cost while you find out whether the newsletter works;
- your paid revenue is likely to stay under roughly $5,000 a year for now;
- you want to write, not administer software;
- minimal design is fine, or preferable.

### Pick beehiiv if:

- paid revenue is already past the break-even, or clearly heading there;
- ads, sponsorships, or paid recommendations are part of the plan;
- you want the publication on your own domain with growth mechanics you control;
- you bring your own audience and do not need rented discovery;
- you would rather pay a predictable fee than a permanent percentage.

### Still unsure?

Answer one question: **do you already have readers?**

If yes, beehiiv's economics and ownership are worth the monthly fee. If no, start free — on Substack, or on beehiiv's Launch plan, which costs nothing up to 2,500 subscribers — and revisit this in six months with real numbers instead of guesses.

## FAQ

### Is Substack cheaper than beehiiv?

Below roughly $5,170 a year in paid-subscription revenue, yes. Above it, beehiiv's fixed fee costs less and the advantage grows with your revenue. That break-even uses beehiiv's $517 annual Scale price at 1,000 subscribers and Substack's 10% share, both verified August 31, 2026.

### Does beehiiv take a percentage of paid subscriptions?

No. beehiiv's Scale and Max plans list a 0% take rate on paid subscriptions. Payment processing fees are separate and charged by the processor.

### Can I move from Substack to beehiiv?

You can export your subscriber list from either platform. Run a test import with a small segment first, and rebuild one real issue before moving an established publication — community reports on beehiiv migrations are mixed enough to be worth an hour of testing.

### Which is better for growing from zero subscribers?

Substack, for most people. Its network and app do real discovery work that a self-hosted publication has to earn. beehiiv's free Launch plan is the alternative if you would rather own the domain from day one.

### Which is better for sponsorships and ads?

beehiiv, clearly. The Ad Network and paid Recommendations ship with Scale, and Max adds a Sponsorship Storefront. That is a revenue path Substack's subscription-share model is not built around.

### Should I use beehiiv's free plan instead of Substack?

It is a reasonable third option: $0 up to 2,500 subscribers, on your own custom domain, with recommendation-network access. You give up Substack's in-app discovery and gain ownership. See our [beehiiv review](/beehiiv-review/) for what the free plan does and does not include.
