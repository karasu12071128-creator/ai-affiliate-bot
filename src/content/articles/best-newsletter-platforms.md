## Quick answer

Pick by revenue model, not by feature count.

- **beehiiv** — best overall when the publication will earn directly, through paid subscriptions, ads, or sponsorships.
- **Substack** — best when you are starting from zero and want no fixed cost while you find out if this works.
- **Ghost** — best when you want to own the whole publication, website and membership included.
- **Kit** — best when the newsletter feeds a creator business that sells products or services.
- **MailerLite** — best when you mostly need to send good email cheaply and the publication is not the product.

If you only take one thing from this page: the platform that is right for a writer building a paid publication is the wrong one for a coach whose newsletter sells a coaching program. Same word, different software.

## 1. beehiiv — best overall for a newsletter that earns directly

beehiiv is built around the idea that a newsletter is a business, and the paid plans reflect that. Scale, listed at **$43 per month** (**$517 billed annually**) at 1,000 subscribers, adds the Ad Network, paid Recommendations, a **0% take rate on paid subscriptions**, automations, and webhooks. Max, at **$96 per month** (**$1,151 billed annually**), adds branding removal, a Sponsorship Storefront, up to 10 publications, and the Send API.

The free **Launch** plan is unusually complete: $0 up to 2,500 subscribers, unlimited sends, a website, a custom domain, and access to the recommendation network.

At our last verified check on August 22, 2026, beehiiv held **4.5/5 from 40 reviews on G2**. That is a small review base and we treat it as such. Reviewers consistently praise the monetization tooling and the coherence of publishing, growing, and earning in one system.

**The case against it:** if you will not use the ad network, paid recommendations, or paid subscriptions, the paid plans are expensive for what you would actually touch — and recent community reports about editor and migration friction are mixed enough to test before you move an established list.

**Choose beehiiv if** the publication will try to earn money on its own within a year. Our [full beehiiv review](/beehiiv-review/) breaks the plans down and names the cases where paying is a mistake.

## 2. Substack — best for starting from zero

Substack charges nothing to publish. Its official page states that **writers keep 90% of their revenue minus credit card fees**, so the platform's 10% share only appears once readers pay you. Card processing is charged separately by the payment processor and varies by country.

The real draw is not the price, though. It is the network. Recommendations between publications and the platform's own reader surfaces do genuine discovery work, which matters enormously when your list is empty and nobody is searching for you yet.

**The case against it:** the growth is partly rented, the editor is deliberately minimal, and the 10% share never ends. Once paid revenue passes roughly $5,000 a year, that share costs more than a fixed subscription elsewhere — the arithmetic is laid out in our [beehiiv vs Substack comparison](/beehiiv-vs-substack/).

**Choose Substack if** you have no audience yet and want to write rather than administer software.

## 3. Ghost — best for ownership

Ghost(Pro) lists **Starter at $18/month billed yearly** and **Publisher at $29/month billed yearly**, both at 1,000 members, with **Business at $199/month billed yearly** for 10,000 members. Ghost takes **0%** of paid subscriptions; payment processor fees still apply. The software is open source, so self-hosting is free if you are willing to run it.

What you get is a publication rather than a mailing list: a real website, themes you control, membership and paid subscriptions, and no platform sitting between you and your readers.

**The case against it:** you are the operator now. Themes, hosting decisions, and integrations become your job, and there is no built-in ad network or discovery feed handing you readers. Starter also holds back staff users, custom themes, and paid subscriptions — for most people the meaningful comparison is Publisher, not the $18 headline.

**Choose Ghost if** the website matters as much as the emails and you want the whole stack to be yours.

## 4. Kit — best when the newsletter sells something else

Kit's free plan runs to **10,000 subscribers** with unlimited landing pages, forms, and broadcasts, plus tagging, segmentation, and selling digital products. **Creator is $33/month** ($390/year) and **Pro is $66/month** ($790/year), both at 1,000 subscribers. Unlimited visual automations and sequences begin on Creator.

At our last verified check on August 22, 2026, Kit held **4.4/5 from 236 reviews on G2** and **4.6/5 from 243 reviews on Capterra** — a much larger evidence base than beehiiv's. Reviewers praise ease of use and useful automation; the recurring complaints are cost as lists grow and limited design flexibility.

**The case against it as a newsletter platform:** the publication is not really a first-class object. There is no ad network, no sponsorship storefront, and no publication-native growth loop. Kit is superb at moving a subscriber toward a purchase and merely adequate at making a publication famous.

**Choose Kit if** the money comes from courses, coaching, services, or digital products. Our [Kit review](/kit-review/) and [Kit vs beehiiv comparison](/kit-vs-beehiiv/) go deeper.

## 5. MailerLite — best low-cost sender

MailerLite's free plan covers **up to 250 subscribers and 2,500 monthly emails**, with three automations, a website, a landing page, and signup forms. **Comfort starts at $12/month** and **Power at $25/month**, with an Enterprise tier for 200,000+ subscribers.

For straightforward, well-designed email at a low price, it remains one of the easiest recommendations on the market.

**The case against it:** the free tier is far smaller than Kit's or beehiiv's, and there is no publication monetization layer at all — no ad network, no subscription take-rate story, no discovery network. It sends email well. It does not make your newsletter a business.

**Choose MailerLite if** cost control is the priority and the newsletter supports a business that earns elsewhere.

## The decision framework

Work through these in order and stop at the first one that describes you.

1. **Does the newsletter itself need to earn money?** If yes, beehiiv, or Ghost if ownership matters more than built-in ad revenue.
2. **Do you have zero audience and zero budget?** Start on Substack, or beehiiv's free Launch plan if you want your own domain from day one.
3. **Do you sell courses, coaching, or products to the list?** Kit.
4. **Do you want the website and membership system to be yours?** Ghost.
5. **Do you just need reliable, cheap email?** MailerLite.

If two of these describe you, pick the one tied to revenue. That is the decision you will regret getting wrong.

## FAQ

### What is the best newsletter platform for beginners?

Substack if you have no audience and want the platform's discovery to help. beehiiv's free Launch plan if you would rather own your domain from the start — it covers 2,500 subscribers at no cost.

### Which newsletter platform has the best free plan?

They are free in different ways. Kit allows the largest list at 10,000 subscribers but is an email marketing tool rather than a publication platform. beehiiv's Launch plan covers 2,500 subscribers and includes a website and custom domain. Substack charges nothing to publish at any list size and takes a share only when readers pay you.

### Which platform takes the smallest cut of paid subscriptions?

beehiiv (0% on Scale and Max) and Ghost (0%) both take nothing. Substack's page states writers keep 90% minus card fees. Payment processing fees apply on all three regardless.

### Is Substack or beehiiv better for newsletter growth?

Substack is better at finding you readers when you have none. beehiiv is better at converting and monetizing readers you already have. The [full comparison](/beehiiv-vs-substack/) includes the break-even math on when the switch pays off.

### Do I need a newsletter platform if I already use an email marketing tool?

Usually not. If your email tool sends what you need and the newsletter is not the product, migrating is a cost with no return. Switch when the publication needs to earn on its own — that is the point where the tooling gap becomes real.

### Can I move between these platforms later?

All of them let you export your subscriber list, so you are not trapped. What does not transfer is platform-native growth: network recommendations and in-app readership stay behind. Run a test import with a small segment before moving an established publication.
