## The short answer

If your short-form video is monetized — a YouTube Shorts channel in the Partner Program, a TikTok with a brand deal, a Reel that sends people to something you sell — **ElevenLabs' free plan is not a legal option for it.** The free tier carries no commercial licence, and anything you publish from it has to credit ElevenLabs in the title.

That single rule decides most of the plan question. Paid access starts at **$6 a month**, which is small enough that the interesting question is not "can I afford it" but "do I need narration at all". A lot of creators reach for an AI voice to fix a problem that is actually a script problem, and pay a monthly fee to keep doing it.

This article is about that decision: what the licence actually says, how much narration the cheap tier really buys, and when to skip voice entirely.

## Who this is for

Solo creators publishing short-form video — Shorts, Reels, TikToks — who are considering an AI voice for narration and want to know the cost and licence rules before committing. If you are producing long-form audiobooks, dubbing at scale, or building voice into a product, your constraints are different and this is the wrong article.

## The licence rule, in ElevenLabs' own words

This is the part that most comparison posts skip, and it is the part that changes what you are allowed to publish.

ElevenLabs' help documentation states:

> The free plan does **not** include a commercial license and cannot be used for any commercial purpose.

And on attribution:

> If you publish content generated using our Services on a free plan or without being signed-in to your account, you must attribute it to ElevenLabs by including 'elevenlabs.io' or '11.ai' in the title.

Two practical consequences:

**Monetized video is a commercial purpose.** If your channel earns — ad revenue, sponsorship, affiliate links, driving traffic to a product — free-tier audio does not have a licence covering that use. This is not a technical limit you will bump into. Nothing blocks the export. You simply will not have the rights.

**The attribution requirement is on the title.** Not the description, not the end card. If you are testing on the free plan, "elevenlabs.io" belongs in the video title.

There is one genuinely useful piece of good news in the same document:

> Content generated during a paid subscription can be used commercially, and indefinitely, subject to our Service-Specific Terms.

So a paid month is not a rental. Audio you generate while subscribed stays licensed for commercial use after you cancel. For a creator producing in batches, that matters: you can subscribe for the month you record a run of videos, then stop, and the files you made are still yours to publish.

## What $6 a month actually buys

ElevenLabs bills in credits, and the conversion for text to speech is stated plainly in its documentation:

> For Text to Speech, one character will cost you one credit.

That makes the tiers easy to reason about in script length rather than in marketing units. Prices and allowances as displayed on the pricing page on 2026-09-11, monthly billing:

| Plan | Price / month | Credits / month | Characters of narration |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Free | $0 | 10,000 | ~10,000 |
| Starter | $6 | 30,000 | ~30,000 |
| Creator | $22 | 121,000 | ~121,000 |

To turn that into videos, you need a rough words-per-character figure. English averages close to five characters plus a space per word, so **30,000 characters is very roughly 5,000 words**. A tightly written 30-second Short runs about 75–90 spoken words. On that arithmetic, the $6 tier covers well over fifty Shorts a month of finished narration.

Two caveats worth holding onto, because they are the difference between the table and reality:

- **Retakes spend credits.** Credits are charged per generation, not per download. Re-running a line because the emphasis was wrong costs the same as the first attempt. If you iterate heavily, your effective script budget is a fraction of the headline number.
- **Unused credits roll over, but only so far.** ElevenLabs' documentation states you "will automatically rollover unused credits up to twice your usual monthly quota, provided you remain on the same subscription." A light month banks capacity for a heavy one, up to a 2× ceiling.

One thing we could not confirm: **which paid tier unlocks which kind of voice cloning.** The pricing page lists Professional Voice Cloning under Creator, but we read conflicting information about whether Instant Voice Cloning is included at Starter, and could not find a documentation page that names the tier requirement. If cloning your own voice is the reason you are subscribing, check that on the pricing page before you pay — do not take a number from this table, or any other article, as the answer.

## Where it saves time in a short-form workflow

The honest case for AI narration in short-form is narrow and real: **it removes the recording session.**

For a faceless channel, the loop without it is write → set up → record → listen back → re-record the three lines you fumbled → edit. The re-record step is the one that quietly eats an afternoon, because it requires the same room, the same mic position, and the same energy in your voice as the original take. Miss any of those and the patch is audible.

With generated narration, a script change is a text edit. That is the whole benefit, and for a creator publishing several videos a week it compounds.

What it does not do is make a weak script work. A flat hook read by a very good synthetic voice is still a flat hook.

## When ElevenLabs is overkill

Three cases where paying for narration is the wrong move:

**Your videos are already working without voice.** Silent, text-led short-form is a complete format, not a compromise. If your retention is fine, adding narration is a monthly cost buying an unmeasured change.

**You are on camera anyway.** If your face and voice are already in the video, a synthetic second voice usually reads as an inconsistency rather than production value.

**You publish occasionally.** The batching advantage above assumes volume. At two videos a month, recording them yourself costs less than a subscription and sounds like you.

We build our own short-form with burned-in subtitles and no voice track — a deliberate choice, made because it removes a whole class of production failure at zero cost, not because we tested narration against it and measured a difference. We have not run that comparison, and we are not going to imply we did.

## The alternative route, if voice is not the constraint

Before subscribing to anything, try the cheaper diagnosis: **publish the next three videos with clean burned-in subtitles and a tighter script, and look at retention.**

If the drop-off is in the first two seconds, that is a hook problem, and narration will not fix it. If viewers stay but the middle drags, that is usually pacing or a script that explains too much. Both are free to fix.

If, after that, the specific thing standing between you and publishing more is the recording session itself, then narration is solving a real bottleneck and $6 is a reasonable price for it.

## Recommended workflow if you do subscribe

1. **Write for the ear before you generate.** Short sentences, one idea each, no clauses that need a comma to survive.
2. **Generate once per line, not per script.** Line-level files make a single fix cheap; a regenerated full script costs the whole character count again.
3. **Batch inside a paid month.** The commercial licence on what you generate persists, so recording a month of scripts in one subscribed period is legitimate and cheaper than subscribing continuously at low volume.
4. **Keep subtitles regardless.** They serve sound-off viewing and they are what you fall back on if you stop subscribing.
5. **Record the real cost after a month.** Count generations, not videos. Retakes are where the budget actually goes.

## Verdict

**ElevenLabs is worth paying for when the recording session is the thing blocking you from publishing, and not before.** The free plan is fine for evaluating the voices and nothing else — the moment the video earns, its licence does not cover you.

If you get there, the $6 Starter tier is the honest starting point rather than the $22 one. Thirty thousand characters is more narration than most short-form creators use in a month, and the rollover ceiling gives you room for an uneven schedule. Move up when you hit the limit, not in anticipation of it.

If you are not sure the recording session is your bottleneck, it probably is not. Fix the script first. That costs nothing and it is the change more likely to show up in your retention graph.
